WHAT THAT IMPLIES FOR THE WAVE

The probe measured whole-step ITL while the LT ran once, so its curve is
decoder(B) + LT(1):

  B    probe ITL   implied decoder-only
  1      1.55 ms      1.06 ms
  8      2.00 ms      1.51 ms      (8x work, 1.42x time)

Two wave designs, at width 8:

  serial per-item LT   1.51 + 8 x 0.49 = 5.43 ms / 8 = 0.68 ms/item   2.3x
  batched LT (W2)      1.51 + 1 x 0.69 = 2.20 ms / 8 = 0.28 ms/item   5.6x

The 5.6x is consistent with our fork's measured 4.9x at width 16, which
has a batched LT. Projected script RTF, taking the codec floor (RTFx 70
= RTF 0.0143) as fixed and scaling only the decoder's share of 0.0357:

  today (S3)            0.0357
  wave, serial LT       ~0.0236   -34% vs S3, -41% vs stock upstream
  wave, batched LT      ~0.0181   -49% vs S3

CONCLUSION: build the scheduler with a serial per-item LT first. It is
worth 2.3x on its own, it needs no change to the LT or the CUDA sampler,
and it proves the per-item cross-attention, per-item alignment, and
per-item seeding that a batched LT would otherwise be debugged on top of.
Batch the LT second, as a pure speedup against a working baseline.
